# Map, environment, UX and performance pass, 2026-11-13 (second)

Result: `flutter analyze` clean; `flutter test` 81/81 (74 before); routing
validator `tools/test_nav.py` 0 failures on the final geometry;
`flutter build apk --release` builds.

## "Collisions" seen from some angles: what they were

The app has no physics. The map is a painted 2.5D scene, and walking means the
routing graph (which `test_nav.py` already holds to the walls: no edge crosses
a wall, every place reachable). What looked like collisions in the owner's
screenshots were **draw-order** and **placement** errors:

| Cause | Seen as | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Statues, palms, the obelisk and the mosque were painted after every wall | a statue drawn over the wall in front of it; palms and the obelisk over a roof; depended on rotation | everything that stands up now shares one depth order (blocks, room-wall stretches, roofed-building pieces, figures) |
| Ptolemaic King and Queen placed in the Grand Hall / Glass Hall wall | the pair standing in the wall (screenshot 1) | moved 22 px into the hall; a test now checks every figure on every plan against walls, units and cases |
| Mesh triangles sorted one by one, with the carpet a hair above its border | the Prayer Room carpet split into green and gold triangles (screenshots 1, 3, 4) | meshes carry draw layers (parts stacked on top paint after); the room was replaced by the mosque below |
| Colossi plinths drawn near-black, axis-aligned | a dark diamond under the statues' feet at diagonal angles | plinths in stone |
| Each roof piece anti-aliased on its own | hairlines fanning across the main building's roof | roof pieces drawn without AA (they join seamlessly); the outer rim keeps it |

Checked by rendering the real canvas from 8 rotations (statues, Prayer Room,
site plan) and 2 opposite rotations (Main Galleries, Tutankhamun), in dark
mode as in the screenshots.

## Mosque

A simple, generic mosque as asked: a limestone hall fitted to the Prayer Room's
outline, as high as the shops around it, an arched door facing the halls, and
one small round dome (about 18 px across on a 42 px-deep room) on a low drum.
It is a symbol: the GEM has no mosque building. The room's outline is a skewed
parallelogram, so the hall is fitted to it and the dome built round in the
plan's own frame (a model made in a unit square would come out as a leaning
ellipse). Built by `tools/build_figures.py` with the project's mesh kit; the
Blender pipeline added in the previous pass is removed, as nothing uses it.

## Site plan (the 2.5D environment)

Compared with the official GEM complex map (gem.eg): the roads, roundabouts,
site ground, car parks and landscaping were all in the plan artwork, but roads
were recoloured to nearly the ground's sand and gardens painted tan over their
green. Now roads are asphalt, landscaping green as in the official legend, the
buildings faced in pale stone (the GEM's alabaster façade). The large open
areas (west plaza, around the Conservation Centre) are open ground on the
official map too: nothing was invented to fill them.

## Performance

Measured, not guessed: frames timed while panning and turning each plan
(desktop CPU, debug build; a phone is several times slower). Scene painting
was the cost: on the site plan 77 % of it was the palms (484 triangles each,
re-projected and re-sorted every frame); on the galleries 98 % the ~1,500 slab
paths.

| Median frame | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| site plan, pan | 5.9 ms | 4.5 ms |
| main building, pan | 3.75 ms | 3.4 ms |
| Main Galleries, pan | 3.2 ms | 2.7 ms |
| main building, turn | 9.1 ms | 8.3 ms |
| Main Galleries, turn | 8.3 ms | 5.5 ms |
| site plan, turn | 6.8 ms | 6.0 ms |

Changes: solids batched into `drawVertices` (one call instead of a path
each); figure triangle order cached per yaw; palms switch to a 72-triangle
model under ~22 px; the depth order's candidate pairs found once per scene with
a per-slab reach (it re-scanned every pair, with the colossus's reach, every
half-degree). This happened while the order grew to include walls and figures.
GPU savings from batching are expected to be larger on the phone but were not
measured (no device available, see below).

## Nearest first

There is no GPS indoors; the app's location is where the visitor says they are.
Distances are walking distances along the routing graph (across floors, around
walls), one search for all places (`MuseumGraph.metersFrom`). Search
suggestions and every filter come nearest first, with the distance on each row;
a place's card shows "3 min · 220 m from you". Recomputed each time search
opens, so it follows the visitor's position; step-free routing is respected.
Without a position: the museum's numbering, and a one-tap prompt to set it.
Unreachable places are listed last without a distance. The attractions have no
geographic coordinates, and straight-line distance would mislead indoors.

## Not verified, and why

* **On a device**: the phone was in use; there is no virtual display here and
  no window was opened on the desktop. Renders come from the real app in the
  test engine at phone size with real fonts, with the plan artwork rasterised
  by cairosvg from the same recolour tables (`MuseumMapCanvas.debugArt`, a
  test-only hook: the SVG cannot be captured under the test clock).
* **GPU/raster time** and memory on a phone.
* The Tutankhamun pan benchmark reads ~0.1 ms: that plan fits the screen at
  that zoom, so panning is clamped and nothing repaints.
